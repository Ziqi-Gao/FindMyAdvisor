# Source Policy

Use this policy for all advisor discovery, professor dossiers, funding checks, and outreach drafts.

## Evidence Labels

- Fact: directly supported by a cited public source.
- Inference: reasonable conclusion from multiple facts, clearly marked as an inference.
- Unknown: important item that could not be verified.
- Risk lead: an unverified concern that needs better evidence before it affects decisions.

## Source Priority

1. Official university, department, program, lab, admissions, and faculty pages.
2. Recent publications from DBLP, PubMed, Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, or publisher pages.
3. Grant databases such as NSF Award Search and NIH RePORTER.
4. Student pages, lab alumni pages, dissertation records, and placement pages.
5. Public talks, slides, course pages, and GitHub repositories.
6. Social posts only as weak signals, never as primary proof.
7. Anonymous comments only as risk leads, never as facts.

## Recency Rules

- Recent publication signal: normally within the last 3 years, unless the field has slower publication cycles.
- Funding signal: active or recently ended grants are stronger than old grants.
- Admissions signal: use the newest admissions page or direct faculty statement available.
- Lab activity: recent students, preprints, seminars, or hiring notes are stronger than static pages.

## Discipline Notes

- CS: weigh top conferences, DBLP continuity, lab students, NSF/industry grants, and direct advising patterns.
- Statistics: weigh methodological overlap, recent journal/preprint work, NSF DMS or interdisciplinary grants, and department PhD structure.
- Biostatistics: weigh PubMed continuity, NIH funding, collaborations, student first-author patterns, and public health or medical school program structure.

## Red Lines

- Do not fabricate fit, publications, funding, students, or admissions openness.
- Do not use Sci-Hub or pirated paper access as part of the workflow.
- Do not send emails without explicit human approval.
